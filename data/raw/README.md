# Input data

This folder holds the inputs of the pipeline, as used for the published results of *The State of Tax Justice 2026* on corporate and company-level tax abuse (chapters 2 and 3, the corporate numbers of the executive summary, annex Tables A1 and A3, the country-comparison figure after the executive summary, and the methodology note). A few third-party files whose terms do not allow redistribution are not included; `fetch_third_party.sh` downloads two of them (see "Not included"). The section "Intermediate files" describes the files in `data/intermediate/`: outputs of earlier pipeline steps, shipped because their own inputs cannot be shipped or are very large.

Paths in the tables are relative to `data/raw/` unless they start with `data/`. A date in a file name (`_2026-08`, `_2026-10-17`) is the month or day the file was downloaded or compiled; a year range (`_2016_2023`) is the period it covers. Data compiled by the Tax Justice Network are licensed under CC BY 4.0. Third-party data stay under their providers' terms, given in the last column and summarised in `LICENSE-DATA.md`.

Together, `data/raw/` and `data/intermediate/` hold 1,148 data files (226 MB), 842 of which are the cached SEC responses in `firm_level/sec_xbrl_2026-10/`. No file is larger than 50 MB. Where a source table or manifest names a stored copy of a document (a PDF or web page), that copy is not included; the URL in the same row says where to find it, where the URL was recorded.

## Conventions

**Currency and prices.** Monetary inputs are in current (nominal) values of each year. The CbCR data and most other inputs are in US dollars. Inputs in national currency or euros (wages, the public reports of individual groups, some source tables) are converted at the World Bank official exchange rate of the year, with ECB rates for years the World Bank file does not yet cover. The estimates are made in nominal US dollars of each year. The figures and tables express money in constant 2025 US dollars, deflated with the US GDP deflator (BEA, FRED series GDPDEF; `US_GDP_DEFLATOR_2017100` and `BASE_YEAR` in `src/config.py`).

**Years.** The OECD labels a country-by-country report by the calendar year in which the parent's fiscal year ends. The first year is wider: the 2016 data cover fiscal years ending between 31 December 2016 and 30 June 2017. A group with a fiscal year ending in March or June 2017 therefore appears in both the 2016 and the 2017 data. The company tables in `extractive/source_tables/` follow the same rule.

**Tax rates.**
- The statutory corporate income tax rate is the OECD's combined rate (central plus sub-central government). Where the OECD has no rate, the Tax Foundation rate is used.
- Hand-collected values in `macro_variables/manual_imputation_values.csv` fill the remaining gaps and override a few rates. For India in 2015–2019 the rate excludes the dividend distribution tax (34.61% in 2015–2017, 34.94% in 2018–2019); the OECD combined rate adds that tax as if all profit were distributed.
- Two rates are set in the code: Martinique takes France's rate, and Malta's rate is reduced to one seventh to reflect its refund system.
- Effective tax rates are built by the pipeline (`src/_etr_construction.py`) from the CbCR data, after profit and tax from extraction are taken out. The rate is tax paid (cash basis) divided by profit before tax of the sub-groups with positive profit, pooled over a five-year window centred on the year, separately for domestic and foreign profit; a domestic cell takes the domestic rate and a foreign cell the foreign rate. Where the pooled profit is below US$100 million, the rate is undefined and the partner's average measured rate is used, or failing that the statutory rate.
- Under unitary taxation, profit a country gains is taxed at its statutory rate. Profit a country loses is valued at its effective rate, the rate it actually collects, capped at its statutory rate; a jurisdiction without a corporate income tax loses nothing (`NO_CIT_JURISDICTIONS` in `src/config.py`).

**Country codes.** Countries are identified by ISO 3166-1 alpha-3 codes. The CbCR data also use aggregate partner codes:
- `W` is the reporter's world total, dropped to avoid double counting;
- `WXD` is "world excluding domestic";
- single letters are continents;
- codes ending in `_O` are the residual "other" countries of a continent.

The pipeline spreads the continental and residual aggregates of countries that do not report partner by partner across eligible markets (step 2). `STLS` ("stateless") marks profit that groups could not assign to any jurisdiction. These rows are dropped and not redistributed, and their amounts are taken off `WXD`. Netherlands Antilles (`ANT_F`) and Bouvet Island (`BVT`) are also dropped.
